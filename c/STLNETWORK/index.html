The case for

1
A dedicated data-centre subsidiary, and the team to run it

STL Networks DC Limited was incorporated on 10 Oct 2026 as a wholly owned subsidiary to establish, acquire, own, operate, manage and market data-centre and telecom infrastructure, and to hold the Department of Telecommunications licences needed to sell connectivity. Management says the aim is a dedicated platform for hyperscalers, data-centre operators and enterprises, with ownership of delivery across the infrastructure and connectivity lifecycle. A new data-centre bench came with it: a COO (2 Jul), a Head of Design & Operations for data centres with 200 MW+ delivered and a Chief Sales & Marketing Officer (both 15–16 Oct).

How management describes the opportunity

·
AI workloads need reliable links between data centres, cloud and enterprises

Interim CEO C. R. Battula: the constraint is 'not only bandwidth' but reliability, route diversity and operational visibility. Hyperscalers want large, high-density infrastructure quickly. Enterprises want to modernise live environments. Government wants resilience, governance and sustained support.

TelecomTalk interview, 30 Sep 2026
·
From a project business to one that operates the infrastructure

The company distinguishes system integration (design, deploy, upgrade) from managed services (ongoing support and optimisation) and says managed-services contracts add revenue visibility. The annual report calls it a 'deliberate pivot' from a government-oriented, project-led model to a hyperscaler- and enterprise-facing lifecycle partner.

TelecomTalk, 30 Sep 2026Annual report FY26
·
Where it will focus in the data-centre stack

Mechanical, electrical and plumbing systems, IT integration and managed operations, where it sees its strongest margins, rather than civil works. Five pillars: full-lifecycle capability, a strong supplier base, a growing in-house data-centre team with experienced leaders from established operators, technology partnerships in liquid cooling and high-density power for AI-ready facilities, and partnerships with hyperscalers, colocation players and large enterprises.

Annual report FY26
·
AI, GPUs and sovereign cloud

The company says it is strengthening data-centre capabilities 'to support GPU-intensive environments' and has built sovereign fibre infrastructure for in-country data movement. It described a plan for 'next-gen edge-to-core interconnect' linking GPU-ready edge data centres with core facilities. This is a stated plan, not a delivered capability.

About the "47 data centres"

The company's FY26 annual report says it has 'established over 47 data centers'. Its listing document (information memorandum, FY25) says it 'supported 47 data centers' and, separately, has 'experience deploying over 45 data centers across India, primarily for public sector enterprises and defence organizations'. The RailTel release says 47 data-centre projects worth over ₹4,000 crore. The figure has been the same since the listing.

How to read it: these are projects the company designed, built, integrated or supported for clients. STL Networks does not own or operate data-centre capacity today (the subsidiary above is brand new). The sites, their size (MW) and their owners are not named, so the number is the company's claim and cannot be checked independently from public documents. The only large facility named is the PowerTel Manesar Tier III site, which was won in Sep 2025 and is still being built.

The business

STL Networks Limited (branded Invenia) was carved out of Sterlite Technologies via NCLT-approved demerger in March 2025 and listed on NSE/BSE in September 2025. It provides fiber network deployment, system integration, IT infrastructure management, data center setup, NOC, and SOC services. Revenue comes almost entirely from domestic government/PSU projects (86% of order book) plus a UK telecom subsidiary that is structurally loss-making.

Our thesis

STLNETWORK holds a ₹5,003 cr order book at 5.22× FY26 revenue, anchored in BharatNet Phase III and government digitalization mandates, and H1 FY27 standalone India business has reached operating profitability (+₹5.10 cr PAT in Q2).

The change that matters is that operating cash flow has swung from -₹71 cr to +₹197 cr in one year as contract assets finally began converting. The market may not yet be pricing in the full working-capital release trajectory. The main risks are that ₹710 cr of receivables are stuck in slow-moving projects, UK subsidiaries are insolvent (net assets -₹228 cr), interest coverage stands at 0.46× requiring cash release every quarter to avoid a debt crunch, and the stock has already tripled in six months.
